# Company-Specific Due-Diligence Questions

Use this reference to derive questions; do not copy the whole bank. Select and rewrite questions so each one connects to a claim, contradiction, missing metric, or investment risk found in the BP.

## Question Design

A strong question contains:

1. The claim being tested
2. A defined period, product, customer, or condition
3. The requested evidence
4. The decision the answer affects

Weak: “Is the technology leading?”

Strong: “Under the same slope, lighting, and grass-height conditions, how do task completion, intervention rate, positioning error, and cost compare with the named competitor? Provide raw logs and test protocols.”

## Industry and Market

- Reproduce the BP market calculation and identify the most sensitive assumptions.
- Build bottom-up SAM/SOM by target customer, sites, units per site, price, replacement cycle, and win rate.
- Explain the budget owner, procurement process, sales cycle, and collection cycle for each target segment.
- Identify direct losses and wins against named competitors and provide evaluation records.
- Explain how the company responds if an incumbent matches performance or cuts price.
- Reconcile conflicting market figures, charts, currencies, and forecast versions page by page.
- Identify the earliest directly comparable product and test any “first” or “only” claim against its launch timeline.
- For every market or use case, identify direct competitors, adjacent entrants, substitutes, customer buying criteria, win/loss evidence, and why the same competitor set should not be applied to other segments.

## Product

- Classify each product as concept, prototype, pilot, paid deployment, standardized product, or mass production.
- Provide price, BOM, margin, delivery time, certification, warranty, and return rate by product.
- Provide test conditions behind headline performance metrics.
- Explain safety and fail-safe behavior for each critical environment.
- Show which features are accepted and used by paying customers.
- Explain why multiple product lines do not fragment engineering and service resources.

## Technology and IP

- Map papers and prototypes to actual production code and hardware.
- Provide latency, frame rate, power, temperature, and accuracy under defined conditions.
- Compare alternative technical routes on one controlled test site.
- Provide failure modes and safe degradation behavior.
- Quantify proprietary data by hours, distance, environments, labels, and ownership.
- Provide patents, FTO work, open-source inventory, licenses, and invention-assignment agreements.
- Explain how the roadmap responds to the latest papers, standards, and competitor releases.

## Team, Identity, and Related Entities

- Verify material degrees, positions, awards, competition results, papers, and prior ventures through primary institutional records.
- Confirm whether founders are the named inventors/authors and whether all relevant rights were assigned to the financing entity.
- Reconcile similarly named companies, brands, founders, and financing announcements; provide registry extracts and relationship diagrams.
- Explain IP, people, contracts, revenue, costs, and data allocation across university, incubator, offshore, domestic, and related entities.
- Provide full-time commitments, invention-assignment agreements, conflicts, related-party transactions, and governance approvals.

## Reliability and Operations

- Provide cumulative autonomous hours, task count, area, uptime, and unattended completion.
- Provide interventions and faults per 100 operating hours with cause distribution.
- Provide MTBF, field-repair time, return rate, maintenance hours, and consumable cost.
- Separate paid, free, dormant, and discontinued deployments.
- Provide OTA history, rollback procedures, safety events, and insurance coverage.

## Customers and Economics

- Provide signed contracts, invoices, collections, usage records, and independent customer access.
- Separate contracts, prepaid orders, framework agreements, intentions, and free pilots.
- Rebuild customer ROI with full lifecycle cost and sensitivity to utilization and maintenance.
- Compare device sale, lease, per-task, and RaaS economics.
- Provide repeat order, renewal, expansion, churn, and refund evidence.
- Reconcile any marginal energy or material cost in the BP to full lifecycle cost, including depreciation, retained labor, deployment, consumables, batteries, maintenance, warranty, insurance, downtime, financing, and collection.
- Provide unit economics separately for device sale, lease, per-task/per-unit, RaaS, software, and component models actually used or planned.
- Provide device-level or project-level accepted output, available days, utilization, price, cash collection, direct cost, intervention, failure, and field-support records for a defined period.
- Show low/base/high sensitivity to utilization, useful life, operator-to-device ratio, maintenance, channel take, price, and collection delay; identify break-even price and utilization.
- Separate customer savings and payback from company gross margin, contribution margin, cash payback, and corporate profitability.

## Supply Chain, Finance, and Ownership

- Provide monthly shipments, revenue, ASP, gross margin, opex, cash flow, and cash balance.
- Identify critical suppliers, single-source items, lead times, price exposure, and restrictions.
- Provide capacity, yield, cycle time, quality control, and working capital for the next scale step.
- Provide cap table, historical financing, preferences, options, related parties, and control arrangements.
- Explain allocation of IP, people, contracts, revenue, and costs across legal entities and institutions.
- Reconcile shipment, revenue, collection, and profit claims to monthly ledgers and bank receipts.
- For each forecast jump, provide the capacity, channel, certification, service, working-capital, and peer-ramp evidence required to achieve it.

## Final Selection Rule

Prioritize questions that can reverse the investment conclusion. Group them by topic, use direct wording, and request evidence. Avoid arbitrary P0/P1 labels unless the user asks for priority ranking.
